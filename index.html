The 1930s

Antonino Vescovo1935–37
About the life and intellectual wanderings of Antoninus, a deeply religious and artistic young man who joins an Italian monastery. Through his monastic life and a penniless pilgrimage, he has whimsical philosophical debates with fellow novices, eccentric scholars, artists, and street performers. Blends medieval theology with allegorical humor. Loosely based on the life of Antoninus of Florence, with historical figures such as Fra Angelico. Unpublished.

The 1950s

Dottyc. –1958rewritten 1961
70,000 words. To V. Kidd 11-16-68.About the turbulent coming-of-age of Dotty O’Toole, a fiercely intelligent and spiritually gifted girl. She escapes a bleak Oklahoma childhood and an abusive family and finds her true home in Galveston, Texas. Amidst intellectual battles with atheistic medical students and the temptations of a free-wheeling beach life, she loses her profound childhood faith and virtue after surrendering to a worldly artist. Though she finds temporary restoration and perfect happiness in her marriage to a virtuous Breton seaman named Charles Peisson, his death at sea forces her into a gritty struggle to rebuild her life and live a graced and independent existence. Probably written 1957–58, when Lafferty returned to writing; may have been written earlier. Rewritten 1961.

His baffling inquiry leads him through a labyrinth of mass illusions, staged inquisitions in the Dungeons of Tertullian, and shape-shifting adversaries, revealing that the entire country is a fragile air-foam forgery orchestrated as part of a trillion-dollar real estate heist. During a bizarre, three-day wandering pageant, she engages with a surreal cast of tycoons, criminals, and cultists, attempting to govern them by preaching a philosophy of rational choice and Final Happiness alongside her grubby planetary counterpart, Cousin Clootie. The irrational and violent population of Earth reacts to the alien teenagers like a deadly planetary allergy, leading to their assassinations and the world’s immediate, callous forgetting of their very existence. No completion date on card; to V. Kidd 10-20-76.
The Elliptical GraveFebruary 21, 1977
SF novel. 66,000 words, 181 pages. To V. Kidd 2-24-77. Published Dan Knight 1989.A group of intellectuals, artists, and students embark on an expedition to the White Goat Valley in Calabria to excavate the earth, the haunted air, and the mind-sphere in search of humanity’s primordial perfection and next mutational leap. Their mission is opposed by jealous, non-human entities called the Stoicheio, who lure the party into an illusory pleasure pavilion that seals them inside an inescapable time stasis. Trapped outside of the cosmos, the expedition members must choose whether to embrace their confinement as a metaphysical cocoon, awaiting a quantum jump that will trigger mankind’s greatest evolutionary transformation.
Dark ShineJune 12, 1977
SF novel. 75,000 words, 204 pages. To V. Kidd 6-14-77.Led by an eleven-year-old prodigy and natural dictator named Rolo N.B. Danovitz, an ambitious eight-person cadre known as the Spaciousness Unlimited Group arrives in the Outer Winds Area with a mission to replace the old world with a new one using mathematical magic and quantum illusions. Despite being surveilled by an intelligence agency called the Cocked Eye BAM, the group manifests the megalithic Spacious City and imprisons their monitors, establishing a domain governed by their own fabricated reality. In a Green Tree, Part 1: My Heart Leaps UpFebruary 28, 1978
1920–1928. 71,000 words. Written January 1 to February 28, 1978. To V. Kidd July 28, 1978.Covers the childhoods of a tightly knit, highly precocious peer group at Crucifixion School in Tulsa, tracing their development from their first days of first grade in 1920 through their eighth-grade graduation in 1928. Throughout these formative years, the children navigate social dynamics, burgeoning romances, and preternatural experiences, including a turnabout party and the drowning of a classmate. This part also introduces readers to the adults in their orbit—including parents, teachers, and the Last Prophet Jan Dooper—as the families undergo changes and several members embark on a grand European tour. Unpublished.
In a Green Tree, Part 2: Grasshoppers & Wild HoneyApril 15, 1978
1928–1942. 79,000 words. Written March 7 to April 15, 1978. To V. Kidd July 28, 1978.Spans the years 1928 to 1942, following James Tyrone and his close-knit peers through their high school, college, and early adult years. During this period, the group forms a society called the Wild Onions, conducts an archeological excavation of reacting clay effigies, and makes it through the Great Depression by launching a variety of businesses. Readers learn about James’s marriage to Angela Ann, her sudden death, his later marriage to Peggy Marie. It concludes with James’s departure for military service in World War II. Mostly unpublished.
In a Green Tree, Part 3: Deep Scars of the ThunderJune 20, 1978
1942–1960. 81,000 words. Written April 24 to June 20, 1978. To V. Kidd August 24, 1978.Spans the years 1942 to 1960, tracking James Tyrone’s military service in the Pacific theater and the subsequent postwar boom that brings massive wealth to the closely knit Tulsa peer group. The work shifts focus to the group’s offspring, the next set of leaves on the tree. This new generation is a mutated cohort that exhibits strange behaviors, alienation, and a susceptibility to subversive or countercultural ideologies. Readers learn of tragedies throughout the 1950s, including the unsolved murder of Alfred F. Pitchblende, the shooting of John Penandrew, and a concluding wave of inexplicable deaths and suicides among several neighborhood teenagers, a version of Lafferty’s “Mud Violet” on this parallel track of fictional reality. Unpublished.
In a Green Tree, Part 4: Incidents of Travel in FlatlandSeptember 5, 1978
1960–1978. 75,000 words. Written July 20 to September 5, 1978. To V. Kidd September 8, 1978.Spans the years 1960 to 1978, the appearance of Flatland and a worldwide unstructuring that brings about cultural decay and the rise of a disaffected, tin-eyed younger generation. As the original central characters age into their later years, they watch their children and grandchildren live in this increasingly chaotic environment of political turmoil, shifting religious doctrines, and new cults. Green Tree concludes with a succession of deaths among the older cohort and the next generation rising to take its place. Unpublished.
Fair Hills of Ocean, Oh!November 29, 1979
185 pages, 63,000 words. To V. Kidd November 29, 1979.When millions of mysterious, gill-breathing Boat People occupy the world’s ports, a secret society enlists the help of their flamboyant, super-intelligent double-agent dolphin, Robert Liffy, to uncover the truth. Their investigation leads them into the oceanic depths to Domdaniel, an ancient, inescapable underwater fortress ruled by the immortal tyrant Carmody Overlark, where they become embroiled in a palace revolution. Filled with fun things: espionage, shape-shifting emperors, and a hollowed-out whale stuffed with stolen jewels. The 1980s

Annals of KlepsisNovember 11, 1980
205 pages, 73,000 words. “Science fiction fantasy adventure comedy romance.” To V. Kidd 11-13-80.Largely set on the eccentric, hallucinatory Pirate Planet of Klepsis, the novel follows historian Long John Tong Tyrone as he explores a universe threatened by extinction through the mathematical Doomsday Equation. Accompanied by a group of one-legged companions, Tyrone navigates through deadly castle intrigues, slave markets, and distorted realities ruled by the time-manipulating ghost of the planet’s founder, Christopher Brannagan. The adventure culminates in a race against time to amend the apocalyptic equation before the death of Quasimodo—the slumbering Horseshoe Nail whose existence anchors the entire cosmos—can trigger the end of all worlds.
Sindbad: The 13th VoyageJune 4, 1982
63,000 words, 187 pages. To V. Kidd 6-21-82. Published 1989 Cholfin / Broken Mirrors Press.Comedic fantasy-adventure that follows Master Mariner Essindibad Copperbottom, a spy from the planet Kentauron Mikron, on his final mission to the magical mirage city of Baghdad on Gaea-Earth to locate the continually reborn Boy-Caliph Harun al-Rashid. The mission is complicated by rival intergalactic spies, shape-changing demons attempting to escape their ancient prison in the center of the Earth, and John Thunderson, a teenager from Chicago who uses an Almost-Anything time-and-space ship to temporarily steal Sindbad’s identity. This journey reaches its peak in a chaotic clash of magic and alternate histories, leaving the true Sindbad miniaturized and trapped inside a six-inch glass bottle where he writes his manuscript while trying to engineer an escape.
Serpent’s EggNovember 16, 1982
201 pages, 64,000 words. Fantasy novel. To V. Kidd November 17, 1982.Set in the year 2035 within a Global Village ruled by the shadowy Kangaroo Court, the novel follows a “Magic Dozen” of hyper-intelligent youths—comprising humans, advanced animals, and ambulatory computers—who were raised in specialized evolutionary experiments. As these super-beings develop world-altering abilities, they are hunted during the final Three Days of Summerset by hybrid assassins known as the Dolophonoi. The invisible government seeks to destroy them out of fear that their group harbors a “Serpent’s Egg”—a dangerously successful creation that must be crushed in its shell before it can upend the global status quo.
East of LaughterMay 4, 1983
220 pages, 72,000 words. To V. Kidd May 5, 1983.Metafictional masterpiece set in a universe that is written into existence by a group of cosmic beings known as the Seven Scribbling Giants. When the greatest of these giants, Atrox Fabulinus, is murdered with his own nine-foot-long quill pen, the world begins to stagger and suffer from a deadly strangulation sickness due to a lack of creative input. To prevent the total collapse of reality, a strange and highly talented ensemble known as the Group of Twelve must negotiate through a landscape of illusions, mythology, and shape-shifting assassins to take up the giants’ mantles and rewrite the future themselves.
Sardinian SummerMarch 15, 1984
Historical fantasy. 214 pages, 82,000 words. Third novel of the Coscuin Chronicles. To Corroboree Press 3-19-84, and copy to V. Kidd.Third part of the Coscuin Chronicles. Follows Dana and the Company Without a Name as they involve themselves in European political events, including the Crimean War and the unification of Italy. Throughout their travels, the characters continue to fight Ifreann Chortovitch, the Son of the Devil, and readers learn his backstory. To alter the course of history, the group executes a plot to secretly replace either Giuseppe Garibaldi, Pope Pius IX, King Victor Emanuele II, or Count Cavour with a royal lookalike known as the Prisoner of Naples. Unpublished.
First and Last IslandMay 5, 1984
Historical fantasy, 1862–1872. 204 pages, 81,000 words. Begun 3-20-84. Carbon to V. Kidd 5-21-84. Original to Corroboree 5-29-84.Fourth part of the Coscuin Chronicles. Dana runs an international maritime trade network from Seven Heads Castle alongside a band of Gypsies, a talking Barbary ape, and a bone-eating sea monster. Amidst escalating political conspiracies and violent clashes with Ifreann Chortovitch, Dana’s close friend Joseph O’Boyle assumes his identity and is condemned to death, allowing Dana to escape across the ocean to the Caribbean island of Basse-Terre. After Dana establishes a new life with his prophetess wife in Guadeloupe while continuing to battle supernatural adversaries, the Chronicles circle back to Ireland, where a young man named Lion Mulville is instructed by a mysterious figure to embark on a similar formative journey. Mysterious matters are resolved. Unpublished.
